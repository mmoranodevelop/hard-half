# Reference Ask

**This-Week Reference Ask — One Customer, One Sentence** — customer (named), why them (one line), the one-sentence ask, format (15-min call / email intro / one quote), date this week, who on our side owns scheduling, what we already did so the customer does zero logistics, thank-you owner. Default: venture; same spine for F500.

Method origin: Forrester customer-advocacy ask (motivation, ease, one coordinated outreach) + HBR/CEB Customer Effort Score (high effort → 81% negative WOM).

If they want a reference-program lecture: one paragraph then produce or stop.

## When to use

- A live pursuit needs one named customer, this week, on one topic
- The pool is being overtaxed (same two logos every time) and this week's pick must rotate
- Sales and CS would otherwise hit the same customer the same day
- The ask is currently "can you hop on?" with no script and no scheduler

## When not to use

- Quarterly value review — [QBR](../../management/qbr/SKILL.md). A reference may be an *output* of a healthy QBR; the ask itself is this skill
- One account in distress — [Churn Save](../../accounts/churn-save/SKILL.md). Do not harvest references from a save
- Year-horizon account design — [Account Plan](../../accounts/account-plan/SKILL.md)
- Proving delivered value as the page — [Value Realization](../../accounts/value-realization/SKILL.md)
- One account's health score — [Account Health](../../accounts/account-health/SKILL.md)
- Named exec on the *prospect* side — [Exec Sponsor Live](../../commercial/exec-sponsor-live/SKILL.md)

## Modes

| Mode | When | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **produce** | Default. Named customer; this-week clock | One-sentence ask + logistics + thank-you + ASK |
| **redline** | They pasted a blast, a CC thread, or a "we need a logo" Slack | Force one customer, one sentence, you-schedule; kill company-panic phrasing |
| **refuse** | Two load-bearing facts missing, or the customer is in an open severity incident | Issues list. Stop |

## Hard rules

1. **Clock is this week.** One named customer, one sentence. Not a program. Not a case-study factory.
2. **Named customer who has consented to be asked** — not a logo scraped from the website.
3. **One sentence in the customer's interest.** Forrester: people like to be needed; they do not like to be used. Customer-focused: "Highlight the impact you've seen… 350 of your peers will see it." Company-focused: "We really need a case study in time for our event." Use the first.
4. **You do the logistics.** Forrester ease: you create the schedule and send reminders. Do not make the customer produce PowerPoint, hunt a slot, or invent a script.
5. **Time-box the format.** 15-min call / email intro / one quote. A reference call with no time-box is a high-effort event.
6. **One coordinated outreach.** Sales and CS do not both hit the customer the same day. Log the use so the next ask is not the same logo tomorrow (Forrester: do not overtax a few; the pool dies).
7. **Do not ask a customer in an open severity incident.** HBR/CEB CES: high-effort interactions drive 81% intent to spread negative word of mouth. A sloppy ask on top of a support fight manufactures a detractor. That is [Churn Save](../../accounts/churn-save/SKILL.md) or [QBR](../../management/qbr/SKILL.md), not this skill. ChartMogul: 5% monthly logo churn ≈ 46% of a cohort gone in a year — burning a referenceable logo is not "just a call."
8. **Brief both sides.** Prospect: what not to ask (pricing, unreleased product). Customer: the one topic.
9. **Thank-you the same week.** Authentic; do not exaggerate benefits or forget them after.
10. **Do not invent consent or quotes.** Holes stay holes.

## Intake

If **two** of 1–4 are missing after one round: issues list, not a fake ask.

1. Named customer who has consented to be asked — load-bearing
2. One-sentence ask (topic + time-box) — load-bearing
3. This-week date and an owner who will do the logistics — load-bearing
4. Confirmation this customer is **not** in an open severity incident — load-bearing
5. Format: 15-min call / email intro / one quote
6. Prospect (if a call) and what they must not ask
7. Last time this logo was asked (rotation) — or "unknown — hole"

## QA (must pass)

1. Named customer, not "someone in manufacturing."
2. One sentence, customer-interest phrasing, time-box.
3. Date this week + logistics owner (we schedule).
4. Open severity = N, or the page stops.
5. Thank-you owner + same-week date.
6. ASK + owner + date.
7. Not a QBR, not a save plan, not an account plan.
8. No invented consent or quotes.
9. One page.

If 1, 2, 4, or 8 fail: do not ship.

## Escalate / stop

- CC a customer on a thread with a prospect and "can you hop on?" with no script, no time-box, no scheduler → refuse. Forrester anti-pattern and CES negative-WOM machine.
- Customer in an open severity incident → stop; [Churn Save](../../accounts/churn-save/SKILL.md) or [QBR](../../management/qbr/SKILL.md).
- Same two logos every week → refuse until rotation is named.
- They want a case-study program or a QBR output list → one paragraph then this week's one ask, or stop.
