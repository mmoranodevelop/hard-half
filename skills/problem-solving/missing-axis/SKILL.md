# Missing Axis

## They are arguing on a line that may not be the line

The default agent, and `first-principles`, search on coordinates the field already named. If the useful coordinate is not in that set, every option is collinear: price vs speed vs accuracy, more of X vs more of Y, camp A vs camp B — the same fight, relocated. A **missing axis** is a measurable coordinate nobody is using. Adding it would split two options that currently look identical.

The leading word is **axis**. You list the coordinates the room already argues on, show the options are silent on something, and name the cheapest observation that would confirm that something is real. "Think different" is the default. A coordinate with a unit, and a test, is not.

This is not `ghost-problem`. That asks whether the *problem* is load-bearing. This one accepts the problem and asks whether the *search space* is missing a dimension. It is not `first-principles`: that rebuilds candidates from the stated invariants — on the current manifold. It is not `issue-tree`: that MECE-splits a governing question you already have. It is not `verified-hard-problem`: that locks one official claim.

## When to stop

- The stated problem may be a decoy → `ghost-problem`
- Real stuck objective, floor and gap on the *current* coordinates → `first-principles`
- Messy why-is-X-down, no suspicion of a missing coordinate → `issue-tree`
- One official unsolved claim to lock → `verified-hard-problem`
- Joint constraints empty on the current sets → `empty-and` (emptiness is not a missing axis, though a new axis can reopen a set)
- Worth building → `value-problem-solver`
- They want a brainstorm of wild ideas with no measurement
- Two load-bearing intake facts still blank after one ask → issues list, stop

## Mode

| Mode | When | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Hunt** | Default. Options feel identical; the field plateaued | Candidate axis + why incumbents are mute + confirmation observation + one ASK |
| **Confirm** | They already named a suspected coordinate | The observation that would kill or keep it |
| **Relocate** | Axis already confirmed | The new search: what splits that did not split before; handoff to `first-principles` on the new manifold |

Infer. Ask only when Hunt and Confirm are equally live.

## Protocol

### 0. Intake — list the axes already in play

Load-bearing (two missing after one round → refuse):

1. The problem in one mechanism-free sentence (if this sentence will not sit still, that is `ghost-problem`)
2. The options or camps the room already has (at least two, even if they hate both)

Write the **current axes**: the coordinates those options actually differ on. If you cannot name them, the room is arguing labels, not axes — extract coordinates or stop.

> **Done when:** problem sentence, option list, and current axes are on the page.

### 1. Show collinearity

For each option, score it on the current axes only. If the options only trade along those axes — more of this, less of that — they are collinear. That is the symptom, not yet the missing axis.

If two options already differ on something the room does not treat as an axis, you may have found it. Name that something as a candidate and skip to step 3.

Read `references/axis-hunt.md` **now** if the camps are ideological, if the field has been stuck for years across *every* known approach, or if you are about to propose "culture" or "quality" as an axis. It holds what counts as a coordinate (a unit, a comparison) and the mute-reasons (unmeasured, not in the ontology, unspeakable, newly possible).

> **Done when:** every listed option is placed on the current axes, and collinearity is shown or broken.

### 2. Propose one candidate axis

One. Not a cloud of themes. An axis has:

- a **name**
- a **unit or comparison** (what would be larger vs smaller)
- a **split**: two options (or two worlds) that look the same on current axes and would look different here

If you cannot write the split, you have a slogan. Delete it and hunt again, or conclude the current manifold stands.

Why incumbents are mute — pick one: not measured; not in the field's ontology; politically unspeakable; newly possible (an instrument or a right that did not exist). A mute-reason is a research clue, not colour.

> **Done when:** one candidate with unit, split, and mute-reason — or an explicit "current manifold stands."

### 3. Cheapest confirmation

The observation that would show the axis exists: two cases that match on current axes and diverge on this one, in the wild or in a cheap probe. Cost, time, what result **kills** the candidate (the cases do not diverge; the unit cannot be scored).

You do not invent the data. You name where it would come from. Missing stays **unknown**.

If the candidate dies, the current manifold stands — hand to `first-principles` if the problem is still stuck. If it lives, **Relocate**: the next search happens on the enlarged space. `first-principles` on the old invariants without the new axis would rebuild the same collinear options.

ASK, exactly one: `run confirmation` / `kill candidate` / `relocate search` / `manifold stands`. Owner, date.

Use `assets/one-pager.md` for the page.

Read `references/worked-examples.md` if they asked you to think outside the box, or if both camps look correct.

> **Done when:** one candidate or a manifold-stands verdict, a confirmation that can kill it, one ASK — and no brainstorm in place of a unit.

## Anti-patterns

- **Theme cloud.** Innovation, culture, quality, alignment — no unit.
- **More-of-the-same-axis.** "Even faster" is not missing; it is the current line.
- **Ghost-mix-up.** You changed the problem instead of the space.
- **Rebuild-on-the-old-map.** First-principles theatre that reproduces camp A vs camp B.
- **Unkillable axis.** No observation would refute it.
- **Five axes.** Hunt proposes one; relocate can add later.
