# Worked examples

Illustrative. Unknowns stay unknown.

## 1. Forecast — fraud check with a short half-life

**Incoming:** "We'll add device fingerprinting. That should stop the promo-abuse."

**Player:** people harvesting the promo, paid per successful claim. Payoff: claims, not "beat our product."

**Update rule:** they see the new check on the first failed batch; they switch to the next unverified channel (rented devices, farmed accounts) within days. **Assumed** speed: < 2 weeks. Hole: exact channel.

**Half-life:** the fingerprint buys until that switch — weeks, not a moat.

**Equilibrium:** none affordable at current promo value vs farm cost. **Budget:** spend per cycle on the next check, stop when cost-to-farm < remaining promo margin, or kill the promo.

**ASK:** `set budget and stop` — do not ship fingerprinting as a lasting fix.

## 2. Autopsy — last quarter's paywall copy

**Incoming:** "They copied our plan names and the extra feature died as a differentiator in six weeks."

**Player:** named incumbent. Payoff: keep their accounts from switching, not invent the category.

**Realized half-life:** six weeks. Forecast you should have written: they can copy naming and a checkbox feature in one release train; they cannot copy the switching cost you did not build.

**Next stance:** stop racing checkbox features. Equilibrium to seek: a switching cost or a standard you both sit on. Or `do not move` on features they can copy inside one train.

**ASK:** `seek equilibrium` or `do not move` on the next checkbox.

## 3. Refuse — moat as a wish

**Incoming:** "Design a moat that lasts."

No player, no payoff, no move. **Refuse.** Issues list: who updates, their payoff, the move whose half-life you want to know. A moat without a player is a slogan. If they name a rival and want a static so-what, that is `competitive-teardown`.
