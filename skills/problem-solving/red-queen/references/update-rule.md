# Update rules

An update rule is **see → speed → next move**. If any of the three is missing, you have a mood, not a rule.

## Fast vs slow players

| Player | Typical see | Typical speed | Failure if you treat them as the other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fraudster / attacker / scrapers | Your shipped control, often immediately | Hours to weeks | You ship a "durable" check |
| Competitor with a release train | Your pricing page, your launch, your customers talking | A quarter to a year | You expect overnight copy, or you expect never |
| Regulator | Harm that is already visible, or a lobby packet | Months to years, then discrete jumps | You model them as a product manager |
| Immune system / biological adversary | Selection pressure | Generations of the pathogen, not of your calendar | You announce a permanent treatment |
| Model-using attacker | Your public surface plus whatever leaked | As fast as they can fine-tune or prompt | You assume last year's attack graph |

Speed is part of the half-life arithmetic. A slow player does not make your move eternal; it makes this cycle long. Write the cycle length.

## When "the market" is allowed

Only if you disaggregate. "The market will respond" is scenario-planning language. For a red queen you need a **kind of actor** (incumbent with a matching product, new entrants on the cheap layer, customers who delay switching, arbitrageurs). Pick the one whose update kills your move first. The others can be holes.

If no actor is faster than your own iteration, say so — the red queen may be *you* racing a ghost. Hand to `ghost-problem` if the other player cannot be named.

## "They will copy us"

Copying is one update, and only if:

- they can see the thing (not a process inside the building)
- they can do it at a cost that beats not copying
- their payoff actually rewards the copy (many incumbents are paid to not cannibalize)

Write those three as yes/no/unknown. A "copy" rule that fails the payoff check is fan fiction. The honest next move might be ignore, block, buy, or regulate — pick one and say why.

## Regulators

Their payoff is rarely "beat you." It is closer to "visible harm stays below a threshold" or "the last inquiry is closed." Their update is often a discrete rule change after a visible event. Your move's half-life may be "until the first public incident," not "until they notice the feature." Write the triggering event, not a vibe about hostility.

## Model-using attackers

Assume they can search your public surface cheaper than you can patch it. The update rule is: each control you add becomes training data for the next bypass. Half-life is then a function of how often they can iterate versus how often you can. An equilibrium here is usually "detected and too costly," not "impossible to attack." If you claim impossibility, that is `verified-hard-problem` or `first-principles` Class A — different skills.
