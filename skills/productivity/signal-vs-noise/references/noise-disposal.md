# Disposing of Noise

Step 4. Moving an item to the Noise column changes nothing. **The disposition is the work** — an undecided item keeps consuming attention from wherever it sits, and a Noise column of forty undecided items is more draining than no list at all, because now the pile is visible.

Every item gets exactly one of four dispositions. No item leaves the session without one.

---

## Delegate

**Requirement: name a person.** "Delegate this" without a name is a deferral pretending to be a decision, and it will still be on the list next week.

A handover that actually stays gone carries four things:

1. **The outcome**, not the steps. Describing the method invites the method being followed instead of the goal being met.
2. **The deadline**, and what depends on it.
3. **What "done" looks like** — the same completion-criterion discipline used on Signal items. Vague handovers come back.
4. **What they can decide alone.** The most-skipped item, and the reason delegated work bounces back as questions. If every judgement call returns to you, you delegated the typing and kept the work.

**Delegation is not free** — it costs a briefing now. That cost is real and it is still almost always worth paying, because it is paid once. The failure mode is delegating something so entangled that explaining it costs more than doing it; when that is true, the honest move is to do it or delete it, not to hand over a mess and call it delegation.

---

## Defer

**Requirement: a date, or a named cycle.** "Later" is not a disposition — it is the item staying exactly where it was.

Two honest forms:

- **A date.** It comes back on the 14th and is re-triaged then.
- **A trigger.** "When the pilot finishes." Legitimate, provided the trigger is observable and someone will notice it.

**Watch the repeat count.** An item deferred three cycles running is telling you something, and it is almost never "the timing has been unlucky". It is one of:

- It is not actually important → **Delete**
- It is blocked by something unnamed → the blocker is the real item
- It is being avoided → usually because it is unpleasant or ambiguous, and naming which one is what unblocks it

Ask directly at the third deferral. People find this uncomfortable and it saves months of the item reappearing.

---

## Batch

**Mandatory Noise.** Payroll, a compliance deadline, a customer escalation, the invoice that must go out. It does not move the mission and it must still happen.

This is the category that makes the whole system survive contact with a real job. A framework that says "ignore everything that is not Signal" is unusable by anyone with obligations, and it gets abandoned in week one — which is why it matters more than it looks.

Three rules:

- **A stated timebox with an end.** "Email 16:00–16:30", not "when I get a moment". Unbounded Noise expands.
- **Never the first hours.** Attention is at its best early and does not come back. Opening messages first hands the best block of the day to other people's priorities.
- **Batched together.** Ten small tasks in one block cost far less than ten interruptions, because the expensive part is the switch, not the task.

**The honest audit:** if the batch is taking four hours a day, the problem is not scheduling. Something in there needs to be delegated, automated, or refused — and that is a conversation worth having explicitly rather than absorbing quietly, week after week.

---

## Delete

The one people avoid, and the one that produces the most relief.

**Say it plainly.** "This is not going to happen" — not "I'll get to it eventually". The vague version keeps the item alive, and a live item costs attention on every future pass, forever.

Candidates:

- Deferred three or more times
- Belongs to a mission no longer being served
- Would have been done already if it mattered
- Arrived from someone else's enthusiasm and was never really wanted

**The permission that helps:** deleting is not failing. It is deciding — and the item was already not being done. Deletion changes nothing about the outcome and removes the ongoing cost of pretending otherwise.

**Where good ideas go.** An excellent idea that is not this mission deserves better than deletion. Park it somewhere outside the working list — a notes file, an ideas backlog, `.out-of-scope/` in the relevant project. The point is that it leaves *this* system. An ideas list you never read is fine; an ideas list inside your daily board is drag.

---

## The completion test

Run down the Noise column. Every line has one of:

- a **name** (Delegate)
- a **date or trigger** (Defer)
- a **timebox** (Batch)
- nothing, because it is gone (Delete)

Any line with none of these did not get triaged, and it will be back tomorrow costing the same attention.
