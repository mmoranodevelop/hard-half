# Thought Leadership Thesis

**One-page thesis** — one disagreeable sentence, the field default it rejects, lived proofs, Monday application, three-year spend, ASK to sign it. Default: venture MD / operator-writer unless they say F500.

Method origin: HBR public bar (expertise, evidence, originality, usefulness); McKinsey public author bar (novel, useful, substantiated). Reconstruct the tests. Do not clone an article.

If they want a thought-leadership lecture: one paragraph then produce or stop.

## When to use

- "What should I be known for?"
- They post/speak and it does not add up to one claim
- Keynote spine, newsletter promise, or three-year beat
- They offered topic candidates — pick one

## When not to use

- Brand system / this-not-that — [Positioning House](../../branding/positioning-house/SKILL.md)
- Slides — [Social Carousel](../../branding/social-carousel/SKILL.md) *after* a thesis exists
- Posting grid — [Editorial Calendar](../../branding/editorial-calendar/SKILL.md)
- Bios — [Bio Pack](../../branding/bio-pack/SKILL.md)
- How it sounds — [Voice Blueprint](../../writing/voice-blueprint/SKILL.md)
- How a single piece is ordered — [Pyramid Principle](../../writing/pyramid-principle/SKILL.md)
- Company manifesto with no named person; deal thesis — [Fundraising One-Pager](../../strategy/fundraising-one-pager/SKILL.md)

## Modes

| Mode | When | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **produce** | Default, or topic candidates | One thesis + parked list + ASK |
| **redline** | Pasted POV / tagline / "I'm known for X" | Same life, sharper sentence; old topic killed |
| **refuse** | No lived proof, or they will not disagree | Issues list. Stop |

## Hard rules

1. **One.** Supporting arguments yes. Three public theses no. Park the rest.
2. Complete-sentence claim a sceptic can deny. "Leadership in the age of AI" is a topic. Fail = slogan.
3. Lived proof: number they earned, mistake they made, method they run, named body of work. "Studies show" is not theirs. Do not invent authority.
4. If an LLM given the industry would emit it, it is not a thesis. Rewrite the sentence; do not add adjectives.
5. Named enemy is a *belief the field holds*, not a person. Monday: what a peer MD does differently.
6. Three-year test: they will still sign it. News-hooks are posts, not theses.
7. Do not write the house, the calendar, or the carousel here.

## Intake

If **what they have done** and **what they deny** are both missing after one round: issues list, not a slogan.

1. Who (one person) — load-bearing
2. What they have actually done (facts, not aspirations) — load-bearing
3. Who they want to be known to (role + situation)
4. Candidates they already hold, if any
5. Proof they can stand behind in a room
6. Positioning house if one exists (do not contradict the roof; do not duplicate it)

## QA (must pass)

1. One complete sentence a sceptic could disagree with.
2. Lived proofs, or labelled holes and **refuse** to claim them.
3. Not LLM-generic for that industry.
4. Monday application is an action, not a vibe.
5. Byline test: delete the name; it still needs *their* proof.
6. ASK has owner + date (sign / rewrite / park).
7. House / calendar / carousel not smuggled in.
8. One page.

If 1, 2, 3, or 6 fail: do not ship.

## Escalate / stop

- No disagreement after one ask → refuse. Offer house or bio, not a fake thesis.
- "AI + leadership" as the idea → demand the reject, or refuse.
- Five things they want to be known for → pick one; park the rest.
- Proof they do not have → narrower claim or refuse.
- Carousel first / promotional product pitch → sibling skill, or strip the product.
